Contractor Insurance in Ontario

Coverage contractors commonly need

Commercial General Liability (CGL)

Covers third-party bodily injury, property damage, and completed operations. CGL responds when a client or bystander is injured, when you accidentally damage property, or when a problem with your finished work generates a claim after you've left the job site. Limits of $2M-$5M are standard - confirm what your contracts require.

Tools & Equipment Coverage

Covers your owned tools, rented equipment, and materials against theft, vandalism, and accidental damage - at your shop, in your vehicle, and on job sites. Limits and deductibles vary widely. High-value equipment should be scheduled individually. Equipment in transit and overnight theft from job sites are common gaps to confirm.

Commercial Auto

Covers work trucks, vans, trailers, and any vehicle used for business purposes. Personal auto policies exclude commercial use - if you or an employee is driving to a job site or carrying tools when an accident occurs, a personal policy may not respond. Certificates naming additional insureds are often required by property managers and GCs.

Installation Floater / Contractor Equipment

Covers materials and equipment you're responsible for during installation - before they become part of the structure. If you're working on a project and materials waiting to be installed are damaged or stolen, an installation floater responds where standard tools coverage may not.

Umbrella / Excess Liability

Higher limits above your underlying CGL and commercial auto. Many municipalities, GCs, and commercial clients require $5M-$10M in total liability. A commercial umbrella policy provides the additional limit cost-effectively. The premium difference between $2M and $5M total protection is often surprisingly small.

Contractor Bonds & Surety

Licence and permit bonds, bid bonds, and performance bonds where required by municipalities, public contracts, or commercial clients. Bonding requirements vary by trade and contract type. We place bonds alongside your insurance program.

Common mistakes contractors make

  • Assuming personal auto covers business use - it doesn't, and Ontario insurers consistently enforce this exclusion
  • Carrying liability limits that are too low for their contracts - many contractors don't realize their GC agreements require $5M until they're asked to produce a certificate
  • Not adding additional insureds properly - a certificate that doesn't meet the contract wording is useless
  • Not checking subcontractor certificates - uninsured subs can create liability that flows back to your policy
  • Underinsuring tools and equipment - tool coverage limits are often set at policy inception and never updated as the business grows
  • Not understanding completed operations exclusions - some cheaper policies limit or exclude coverage for work already performed
  • Buying only on price - the cheapest CGL policy often has the narrowest definition of covered operations or the most exclusions
  • Waiting until the day a certificate is required - coverage needs to be in place before the work starts, not after
  • Failing to update the policy when revenue, vehicles, or operations change - undisclosed revenue changes and unscheduled vehicles are common coverage gaps

Certificates, contracts, and additional insureds

Many contractors need certificates quickly for property managers, municipalities, general contractors, landlords, builders, or commercial clients. A certificate confirms your coverage and, when required, names the requesting party as an additional insured on your policy.

We handle certificate requests and additional insured endorsements regularly - usually same-day. When a new contract requires specific wording, limits, or endorsements, we review the requirements, confirm whether your current policy responds, and arrange the correct documentation. Call us when you win the contract, not the morning work begins.
