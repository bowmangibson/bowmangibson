Small business insurance in Ontario

Built for Ontario business owners
who need proper commercial advice.

Many small businesses discover coverage gaps only after a claim - not because the owner doesn't care, but because standard packages are built for average risks and most businesses aren't average. A proper commercial review identifies the specific exposures that apply to your operation and makes sure coverage actually responds when something goes wrong.

Coverage areas
Commercial General Liability (CGL)

Third-party bodily injury and property damage arising from your operations. The baseline coverage for nearly every business - and the one most commonly underestimated in terms of required limits.

Commercial Property

Your business contents, equipment, and owned or leased premises. Replacement cost coverage can help reduce the financial impact of a covered loss, subject to policy limits, deductibles, exclusions, and conditions.

Business Interruption

If a covered loss forces you to close temporarily, business interruption insurance replaces lost income and covers ongoing fixed costs during the period you're unable to operate.

Cyber Liability

Small businesses are increasingly targeted by ransomware, phishing, and data breaches. Cyber coverage pays for incident response, customer notification, regulatory response costs, where covered and legally insurable, and business losses.

Commercial Auto

Personal auto policies may restrict or exclude certain business uses. If a vehicle is used for business - client visits, deliveries, job sites - you may need business-use rating, endorsements, or a commercial auto policy, depending on the insurer; a personal auto policy may not respond if business use was not disclosed or is excluded.

Why use a broker for commercial insurance

Standard commercial packages are written for the middle of the market. Most small businesses have at least one exposure that falls outside what a package policy covers well - whether it's a specific liability, a unique piece of equipment, a contractual requirement, or a gap in business interruption. An independent broker reviews your actual operation and identifies those gaps before a loss finds them for you.
