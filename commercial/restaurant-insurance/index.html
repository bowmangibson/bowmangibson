Restaurant insurance built around your kitchen, not ours.

Who this covers

Built for Ontario business owners
who need proper commercial advice.

Restaurants have a complex risk profile. A single kitchen fire can generate property, business interruption, and liability claims simultaneously. A slip-and-fall in a dining room is one of the most common commercial liability claims in Ontario. Liquor liability is a separate and significant exposure that requires its own treatment. An independent broker structures your restaurant policy to account for all of these exposures - not just the obvious ones.

Coverage areas
Premises & Operations Liability

Covers slip-and-fall claims, customer injuries, and property damage arising from your restaurant operations. The most frequent source of restaurant liability claims in Ontario.

Liquor Liability

If you serve or sell alcohol, you have a specific liquor liability exposure under Ontario's Liquor Licence Act. Standard CGL policies often exclude or limit this coverage - it typically needs a separate endorsement or policy.

Commercial Property & Equipment

Kitchen equipment, walk-in coolers, dining room furnishings, and leasehold improvements. Equipment breakdown coverage is particularly valuable for restaurants where refrigeration and cooking equipment failure can cause immediate losses.

Business Interruption & Food Spoilage

If a covered loss forces you to close, business interruption insurance covers your lost income and fixed costs during the closure period. Food spoilage coverage handles inventory losses from equipment breakdown or power failure.

Why use a broker for commercial insurance

Restaurant insurance is not a commodity. The right policy depends on your hours, your menu, your lease requirements, your liquor license status, your employee count, and your event exposure. A broker who understands food service operations identifies the specific gaps that a standard commercial package misses.
