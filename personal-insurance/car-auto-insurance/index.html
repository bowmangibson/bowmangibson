Third-Party Liability Mandatory

Covers you if you injure someone or damage their property. Minimum is $200K but we recommend $1M-$2M. Medical and legal costs can exceed the minimum in seconds.

Accident Benefits (SABS) Mandatory

Pays income replacement, medical, rehabilitation, and attendant care costs regardless of fault. Default limits are often inadequate - optional upgrades are available and underused.

Direct Compensation (DCPD) Mandatory

Allows you to collect directly from your own insurer for damage caused by another Ontario driver. Faster resolution, no subrogation fights.

Collision Coverage Optional

Covers your vehicle for damage from a collision, regardless of fault. Usually required by your lender or leasing company. Deductible is your choice.

Comprehensive Coverage Optional

Covers non-collision damage: fire, theft, vandalism, hail, fallen objects, wildlife. Worth the premium on any vehicle with meaningful residual value.

Family Protection (OPCF 44R) Recommended

Protects you and your family if hit by an underinsured or uninsured driver. One of the most important optional coverages and often overlooked.

Occasional drivers, young drivers, and household members

Every licensed driver in your household must be disclosed to your insurer - even if they rarely use the vehicle. Ontario insurers consistently deny claims arising from undisclosed household drivers, citing material misrepresentation. This is one of the most common and most avoidable coverage problems we see.

  • Young drivers (G1/G2) - adding a young driver typically increases your premium. However, not disclosing them creates a far bigger problem: a denied claim at the worst possible time. If your teenager occasionally uses your vehicle, they need to be listed
  • Occasional drivers - a family member who drives the car two or three times a month is still a household driver. They should be listed as an occasional driver on the policy
  • New drivers in the household - when someone in your home gets their licence, notify your broker immediately. A brief window of non-disclosure can have lasting consequences
Business use warning

If you use your personal vehicle for any business purpose - visiting clients, making deliveries, driving to job sites, picking up materials - a personal auto policy may not cover an accident that occurs during that use. This is one of the most consistently enforced exclusions in Ontario insurance.

If your vehicle is used for business, tell us. A commercial auto endorsement or a separate commercial auto policy is usually required. The additional cost is often modest compared to the risk of an uncovered claim.

How Ontario auto insurance rates are calculated

Ontario has the highest auto insurance rates in Canada - an average of over $2,000 per year. Understanding what drives your rate is the first step to managing it. Insurers weigh dozens of factors including:

  • Your driving record - convictions, at-fault accidents, and claims history typically affect your rate for 6 years
  • Your vehicle - make, model, year, and how it's used (commuting vs. pleasure)
  • Your location - postal code affects rates significantly. Urban centres like Oshawa and Whitby have different risk profiles
  • Your coverage selections - mandatory coverages are fixed; your choices on Collision, Comprehensive, and optional benefits drive the rest
  • Your deductible - higher deductibles mean lower premiums, but you bear more risk on smaller claims

As an independent broker, we shop your profile across our 30+ markets and specialty programs simultaneously and place your coverage with whoever offers the best combination of price and claims service for your situation. That's something a direct insurer or captive agent simply cannot do.

Optional coverages worth considering

Beyond the mandatory coverages, Ontario drivers can add several optional protections that are often underutilized:

  • Accident Forgiveness - prevents your first at-fault accident from raising your premium
  • OPCF 20 (Loss of Use) - pays for a rental vehicle while yours is being repaired
  • OPCF 27 (Liability for Damage to Non-Owned Automobiles) - extends your liability to rental cars
  • Increased Accident Benefits - the default benefits under SABS are modest; upgrades to income replacement and medical limits are worth serious consideration
  • Family Protection Coverage (OPCF 44R) - protects you and your family if you're in a collision with an underinsured driver
