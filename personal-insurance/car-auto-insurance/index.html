Occasional drivers, young drivers, and household members

You should disclose all licensed household members and anyone who may regularly or occasionally drive the vehicle. Insurer rules vary, but failing to disclose drivers or vehicle use can create serious coverage issues, including potential denial of a claim. This is one of the more common and avoidable coverage problems we see.

  • Young drivers (G1/G2) - adding a young driver typically increases your premium. However, failing to disclose a household driver can create serious coverage issues, including potential denial of a claim. If your teenager may use your vehicle, they should generally be disclosed
  • Occasional drivers - a family member who drives the car occasionally may still need to be disclosed as a household or occasional driver. Insurer rules vary, so check with a licensed broker
  • New drivers in the household - when someone in your home gets their licence, notify your broker immediately. A brief window of non-disclosure can have lasting consequences
Business use warning

If you use your personal vehicle for any business purpose - visiting clients, making deliveries, driving to job sites, picking up materials - a personal auto policy may not cover an accident that occurs during that use. This is one of the most consistently enforced exclusions in Ontario insurance.

If your vehicle is used for business, tell us. A commercial auto endorsement or a separate commercial auto policy is usually required. The additional cost is often modest compared to the risk of an uncovered claim.

How Ontario auto insurance rates are calculated

Ontario has the highest auto insurance rates in Canada - an average of over $2,000 per year. Understanding what drives your rate is the first step to managing it. Insurers weigh dozens of factors including:

  • Your driving record - convictions, at-fault accidents, and claims history typically affect your rate for 6 years
  • Your vehicle - make, model, year, and how it's used (commuting vs. pleasure)
  • Your location - postal code affects rates significantly. Urban centres like Oshawa and Whitby have different risk profiles
  • Your coverage selections - mandatory coverages are fixed; your choices on Collision, Comprehensive, and optional benefits drive the rest
  • Your deductible - higher deductibles mean lower premiums, but you bear more risk on smaller claims

As an independent brokerage, we compare your profile across a range of available Ontario auto markets and present options we believe are suitable based on a balance of coverage, price, insurer appetite and underwriting eligibility. That's something a direct insurer or captive agent simply cannot do.

Optional coverages and endorsements

Beyond the mandatory coverages, Ontario auto policies may offer optional endorsements and coverage choices. These options should be reviewed with a licensed broker based on your drivers, vehicles, household, travel habits, financial circumstances and risk tolerance. Examples may include:

  • Accident Forgiveness - may help prevent a first at-fault accident from affecting your premium, subject to insurer rules and eligibility
  • OPCF 20 (Loss of Use) - may help with the cost of a rental vehicle while yours is being repaired, subject to policy wording and limits
  • OPCF 27 (Liability for Damage to Non-Owned Automobiles) - may extend certain liability coverage to vehicles you don't own, such as rentals, subject to policy wording
  • Increased Accident Benefits - optional buy-ups to income replacement and medical limits may be available; the default SABS benefits and available enhancements can change and should be reviewed
  • Family Protection (OPCF 44R) - may respond if you're in a collision with an underinsured or unidentified driver, subject to policy wording and limits

Examples above are general examples only and are not recommendations for every client. Availability and suitability vary by insurer, policy wording, and individual circumstances. Ontario auto rules and optional coverages can change - review your options with a licensed broker before making any changes.

Coverage details
Third-Party Liability

May respond if you injure someone or damage their property, subject to policy wording and limits. The statutory minimum is $200K; higher limits may be available and can be reviewed with a licensed broker based on your circumstances.

Accident Benefits (SABS)

May help with income replacement, medical, rehabilitation, and attendant care costs, subject to policy wording and limits. Optional buy-ups to the standard limits may be available; the standard benefits and available enhancements can change and should be reviewed with a licensed broker.

Direct Compensation (DCPD)

May allow you to collect directly from your own insurer for damage caused by another Ontario driver, subject to policy wording. Often means faster resolution.

Collision Coverage

May respond to damage to your vehicle from a collision, regardless of fault, subject to policy wording and your deductible. Often required by a lender or leasing company.

Comprehensive Coverage

May respond to non-collision damage such as fire, theft, vandalism, hail, fallen objects, or wildlife, subject to policy wording and your deductible.

Family Protection (OPCF 44R)

An optional endorsement that may respond if you're in a collision with an underinsured or unidentified driver, subject to policy wording and limits. A licensed broker can review whether this option may be relevant to your circumstances.
